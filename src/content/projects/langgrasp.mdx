## Problem & constraints

A language-guided pick is only interesting if you can say *why* it picked that object, and only
trustworthy if something independent can stop it. The constraint set was deliberately hostile:
no robot, no depth camera, no Jetson, and a single shared NVIDIA L4. So the target was not a
demo video - it was a stack where every stage is inspectable, every number has hardware beside
it, and the safety argument is written as a hazard analysis before the code.

That forced three decisions. Grounding runs **once** per command rather than per frame, because
at ~297 ms it is the whole latency budget. Segmentation is a small fine-tuned model exported to
TensorRT rather than a foundation segmenter, because it has to stay under the 10 Hz control tick.
And the safety monitor is a separate process-level gate with a latched e-stop, not an `if`
statement inside the controller.

## Results

The headline is **115 of 120** stratified trials placing the requested object, split evenly across
seen objects, unseen objects and rephrased commands. Grounding itself was correct on 115 of 119
commands, so almost every end-to-end failure is a grounding failure rather than a control failure -
which is the useful thing to know, because grounding is the part that is swappable.

## Ablations

Two components each carry 9 placements. Removing the HSV colour check drops the pipeline from
115/120 to **106/120**; removing the YOLO mask costs the same. They fail on different things,
though: without the mask, screwdrivers fall from 39/41 to 30/41, because a box around a
screwdriver contains mostly table, and PCA over a box is not a grasp.

The most useful result came out of the interface rather than the benchmark. Plotting the
executor's commanded joint velocities against the safety monitor's 1.5 rad/s limit shows the
executor peaking at **21.8 rad/s**. Enforcing the limit takes the pipeline from 120/120 to
73/120 on that task - so the limit is real, the executor is wrong, and the honest thing to ship
was a GUI that reports the clipping instead of a monitor quietly configured not to fire.
