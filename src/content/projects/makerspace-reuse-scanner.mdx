## Problem & constraints

A makerspace throws away parts that are still good because nobody can tell, at the bin, what a
thing is made of and where it should go. The constraint that shaped everything was **on-premise**:
no cloud API, no data leaving the building, and a corpus that is bilingual because the disposal
rules are written in German and the people reading them are not all German speakers.

That rules out the easy version. A local 8B model has a much smaller margin for retrieval error
than a frontier API does, so retrieval quality is the whole system - and a wrong answer about a
hazardous material is worse than no answer, which makes refusal a feature rather than a failure.

## Results

On 25 hand-verified questions the assistant cited the expected document **20/20** times, declined
**3/3** out-of-corpus questions, and grounded **2/2** stock questions on the live inventory
snapshot rather than answering from memory. Median latency is **2.44 s** per question on a warm
`llama3.1:8b` at k=4, and answers come back in the language the question was asked in.

The detector reaches **mAP50 0.410** on the held-out test split across 9 classes - a number that
is only meaningful with its caveats attached, which is why they are in the limits panel rather
than a footnote.

## Ablations

The retrieval change was driven by a measured failure, not a hunch. With vector-only retrieval,
questions containing rare literals - `Kamin`, `PVC` - retrieved the wrong chunks, and the model
filled the gap by **inventing a hazard claim**. Dense embeddings do not reliably preserve a rare
token that carries all the meaning of the query.

Adding IDF-weighted Postgres full-text search alongside the dense vectors fixed those cases. The
useful lesson is the ordering: the hybrid retriever exists because there was an evaluation that
could catch the failure, not because hybrid retrieval is fashionable.
